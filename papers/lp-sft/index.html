The question

How can supervised fine-tuning learn the target token without erasing the useful local preferences already encoded by the base model?

↗

01 / Abstract

Preserve the neighborhood, learn the target.

Standard cross-entropy fine-tuning increases the probability of the observed label token, but it does not control how probability mass is redistributed over other plausible alternatives. LP-SFT treats the frozen base model as a local structural reference: it selects a small top-K support, removes the supervised target from that support, and applies a locally normalized KL objective to preserve relative preferences among the remaining alternatives.

The paper connects this objective to a multimodal entropy structure in next-token distributions. Across mixed-domain and domain-specific experiments, local preservation improves the balance between pass@1 accuracy and pass@k diversity while reducing destructive drift.

02 / Objective intuition

What should fine-tuning preserve?

CE fits the target. LP-SFT also protects the relationships among plausible non-label tokens.

03 / Method

A local reference, not a full-vocabulary anchor.

The base model is used once to cache the small support needed by the preservation term. Fine-tuning then keeps the usual trainable forward pass.

04 / Evidence map

Overall drift is not the same as harmful drift.

Local KL and margin diagnostics separate useful sharpening from destructive contraction.

06 / Takeaways

Three details carry the method.

  1. 01
    Local, not global.

    Only the base model's small plausible neighborhood is preserved, so the objective stays light.

  2. 02
    Remove the target.

    The supervised token belongs to cross-entropy; excluding it avoids conflicting gradients.

  3. 03
    Normalize within the support.

    Local KL keeps relative preferences while leaving total support mass free to adapt.
