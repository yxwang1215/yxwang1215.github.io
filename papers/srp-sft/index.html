Abstract

Preserve the relation that matters, and leave room to adapt.

Supervised fine-tuning changes logit margins among non-target tokens at teacher-forced prefixes. Matching a local reference distribution keeps those probabilities and also penalizes margin expansions. SRP-SFT asks whether a soft auxiliary can preserve selected local non-target margin relations without freezing that distribution. A frozen reference selects top-K non-target pairs; a hinge penalty activates only when the student falls below a reference-scaled lower bound. Once the bound is met, the auxiliary stops acting.

00 Why this paper

Why previous objectives fall short

CE supervises the observed target, while distribution matching can over-constrain useful adaptation. SRP-SFT protects only the selected directional relations that should not collapse.

01 Method

From matching distributions to preserving margins

The target token remains the job of CE. SRP-SFT only repairs selected teacher-preferred alternative pairs when their margin becomes too small.

A

Reference-guided set

Select the frozen model's top-K non-target alternatives at the same teacher-forced prefix.

B

Teacher-preferred pairs

Keep one orientation for every strictly ordered pair, up to K(K−1)/2 constraints.

C

One-sided hinge

Penalize only violations of the scaled lower bound; expansion beyond the bound is free.

04 What to remember

Three design choices define SRP-SFT.

01

Exclude the target

Cross-entropy learns the observed response token; the auxiliary only acts on non-target alternatives.

02

Keep the direction

The frozen reference orients each pair, so the student is asked to avoid losing a preferred relation.

03

Stop at the bound

Once the scaled margin is met, expansion is allowed. The auxiliary does not force exact distribution matching.
