Why audio needs its own KV policy

Head specialization. Only a subset of attention heads reliably connects decoded words to audio. Uniform per-head cache budgets are inefficient.

Temporal continuity. Raw top-K token selection clusters in narrow time windows and loses global acoustic context.

AudioKV: align, allocate, smooth

1. Identify audio-critical heads. Align word spans using WhisperX and compute per-head top-K attention hit rates.

2. Allocate cache and apply Spectral Score Smoothing. Suppress high-frequency score noise with FFT-based global spectral filtering, redistributing selection across the acoustic sequence.

Method overview

Stable under aggressive compression

Across Qwen and Gemma audio-language models, AudioKV preserves performance with KV retention ratios of 80%, 60%, and 40%. At 40% retention, Qwen3-Omni-30B on LibriSpeech-long retains 97.8% accuracy, compared with 98.3% using the full KV cache.

Competing cache-selection methods can experience severe degradation in long-form ASR.

Failure mode: repetition collapse

On a 232.62-second LibriSpeech sample with a 512-token KV budget, SnapKV reaches 93.62% WER while AudioKV reaches 1.09% WER.

Better selection preserves the discourse-level context necessary to avoid repetitive generation.

Takeaways

01 · Allocate by acoustic relevance.

Word-aligned attention hits identify the heads that ground decoded speech.

02 · Smooth globally, not locally.

FFT-based SSS suppresses score noise while preserving global semantic trends.

03 · Avoid catastrophic collapse.

Tight KV budgets remain viable for reliable long-form audio inference.
